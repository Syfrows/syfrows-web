Product Engineering

An Offshore Product Team That Works Like an In-House One

Syfrows builds web applications and SaaS products for companies in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and the UAE. You get a small, senior team on a fixed scope with a named deliverable, not a pool of rotating contractors.

Who this is for

This page is for a specific situation. You have a product, or a clear idea of one, and you need engineering capacity that costs less than a US or UK hire without the usual offshore problems: work that arrives at 3am with no context, a project manager who relays messages instead of answering questions, and scope that quietly expands until nobody knows what the bill will be.

It tends to fit companies that are:

  • A funded or bootstrapped SaaS company that needs to ship faster than its current headcount allows.
  • An established services business turning an internal tool into a product.
  • A founder with validated demand and a prototype that will not survive real customers.
  • A company whose existing agency delivered something that works but cannot be maintained.

What we build

Web applications and SaaS platforms

Multi-tenant applications with authentication, role-based permissions, billing, an admin surface and the reporting your customers will ask for in month three. Built as a codebase your own engineers can take over, with readable structure and no proprietary framework holding you hostage.

Customer portals and internal tools

The dashboard your clients log into, the operations console your team runs the business on, the integration layer between the systems you already pay for. Unglamorous work that usually pays for itself quickly.

API and integration work

Connecting your product to the systems your customers already use: CRMs, payment providers, ERPs, logistics platforms. It also means exposing your own API so that your customers can do the same.

Rescue and modernisation

Taking over a codebase someone else left behind. We start with a written assessment of what is there, what is salvageable and what honestly is not, before anyone commits to a rebuild.

How we work across time zones

This is the question every US and UK buyer asks, and it deserves a direct answer rather than a promise of “24/7 availability” that no small team can actually keep.

  • Working day. 10:00–19:00 IST, with the team available to 22:00 IST for client calls. That is a live overlap with UK and European mornings and with US Eastern mornings. For US Pacific we schedule calls at the start of your day.
  • One shared channel. A dedicated Slack or Microsoft Teams channel with the engineers doing the work. You ask a question and the person who wrote the code answers it.
  • A weekly written update. Every week: what shipped, what is next, what is blocked and what we need from you. In writing, so you can forward it rather than re-explain it.
  • Your contracts and your NDA. We sign your NDA before any commercially sensitive material changes hands, and we work to your contract rather than insisting on ours.
  • Your repository, your accounts. Code lives in your GitHub organisation from day one. Infrastructure runs in your cloud account. Nothing of yours is held in ours.

What we are not

Being clear about this saves everyone a call.

  • We are not a body shop. We do not place developers into your team to be managed by you.
  • We are not the cheapest option, and we will lose on price to a marketplace freelancer every time.
  • We do not take on work below roughly $3,000. Below that the overhead of doing it properly costs more than the project is worth, and you would be better served by a specialist freelancer.
  • We do not take on regulated clinical, financial-trading or defence work that requires certifications we do not hold.

Questions buyers ask

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit. The repository is in your organisation and the intellectual property transfers to you under the contract, not on final payment.

What happens if we want to bring it in-house?

That is a normal outcome, not a failure. We document as we go and run a handover: architecture notes, environment setup, a walkthrough with your incoming engineers, and a defined support window after they take over.

How do we know the quality is there before committing?

Start with the discovery engagement. It is a small, fixed-price piece of work with a concrete deliverable, a technical plan you own regardless of what you decide next. You will know from that document whether we understand your problem.

Who actually does the work?

Syfrows is a small team, between two and nine people depending on load. You will meet the people assigned to your project before the contract is signed, and they do not change mid-project without a conversation.
