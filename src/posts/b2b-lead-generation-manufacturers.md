Manufacturers have long relied on trade shows, distributors and referrals. Those still matter, but today most engineers and buyers shortlist suppliers online before they ever pick up the phone. If your capabilities are hard to find or hard to understand, you are not on the list.

This guide covers what works for B2B manufacturers, including PCB fabrication and assembly, EMS, components and industrial products.

## 1. Build capability pages engineers can use

Your website is a technical sales document. Each core capability deserves its own page with specifications buyers check first:

- Processes, materials, tolerances, layer counts or machine capacity.
- Certifications (ISO, IATF, AS9100, UL, RoHS) and quality processes.
- Minimum order quantities, lead times and prototype vs production options.
- Industries served and typical applications.
- A short RFQ form with file upload for drawings or Gerber/BOM files.

## 2. SEO for technical search terms

Engineers search with precise phrases like "rigid-flex PCB manufacturer", "low volume PCB assembly" or "box build EMS India". Target these long-tail terms with dedicated pages and helpful guides. They have lower volume than generic keywords but far higher intent.

## 3. LinkedIn for reach and trust

LinkedIn lets you reach design engineers, purchasing managers and founders by job title, industry and company size. Combine a consistent company page (process videos, factory updates, technical tips) with targeted ads and founder or sales-team posts.

## 4. Google Ads for high-intent demand

Bid on specific product and process terms, exclude job seekers and students, and send traffic to the matching capability page rather than the homepage. Track RFQ submissions and calls as conversions so the campaign optimises for real enquiries.

## 5. Targeted outreach to named accounts

Build lists of companies that match your ideal customer (industry, size, region, products) and reach the right roles with short, specific emails and LinkedIn messages. Lead with something useful, such as a design-for-manufacturing tip or a quick-turn prototype offer, rather than a generic company introduction.

## 6. Follow-up and CRM

B2B cycles are long. Log every RFQ in a CRM, respond within hours, and nurture contacts who are not ready yet with case studies and technical content. Many orders come from leads that first enquired months earlier.

## A simple 90-day plan

| Weeks | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Tracking setup, capability pages, RFQ form, Google Business Profile. |
| 4–8 | Google Ads on top capability terms, LinkedIn page content, first outreach lists. |
| 9–13 | SEO guides, LinkedIn ads, review cost per RFQ and double down on what works. |
