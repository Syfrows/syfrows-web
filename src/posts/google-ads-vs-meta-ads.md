Google Ads and Meta Ads are the two biggest advertising platforms in the world, but they work in very different ways. Choosing the right one, or the right mix, can make the difference between profitable growth and wasted budget.

## How Google Ads works

Google Ads shows your ads to people who are actively searching for something. If someone types “website development agency” or “buy industrial sensors”, they already have a need. This is called **intent-based** advertising.

- Best for: capturing existing demand, high-value services, B2B and urgent needs.
- Formats: Search, Performance Max, Display, YouTube and Shopping.

## How Meta Ads works

Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) shows your ads to people based on who they are and what they are interested in, even if they are not searching yet. This is **interest-based** advertising, and it is great for creating demand.

- Best for: e-commerce, consumer brands, new products, retargeting and building awareness.
- Formats: image, video, carousel, Reels, Stories and lead forms.

## Side-by-side comparison

|  | Google Ads | Meta Ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Targeting | Search keywords and intent | Interests, demographics, lookalikes |
| Buyer stage | Ready to buy or compare | Discovering and considering |
| Cost per click | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Lead quality | Usually higher intent | Varies, needs good qualification |
| Creative needs | Strong copy and landing pages | Strong visuals and video |

## Which should you choose?

- **B2B and high-value services:** start with Google Search, then add LinkedIn and Meta retargeting.
- **E-commerce and consumer brands:** Meta usually drives scale, with Google Shopping and Search for high-intent buyers.
- **Local services:** Google Search and Google Business Profile first.

## The best answer is usually both

Most growing businesses get the best results by using Google to capture demand and Meta to create and retarget it. The key is accurate conversion tracking, so budget moves to whichever channel brings customers at the lowest cost.
